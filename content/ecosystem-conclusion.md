If you've followed the tutorial this far, well done! You've now gained a broad
overview of the key techniques that enable us to develop unikernels and some of
our libraries. Of course, the ecosystem we offer is much broader: not only
because we're trying to produce other unikernels (which often involves
developing other protocols), but also because other people are following and
adapting our work to suit their own needs.

The community is therefore (thankfully) not confined to our co-operative, but
the co-operative provides the fundamental building blocks for developing
unikernels. This is why we maintain [Solo5][solo5], [mkernel][mkernel],
[Miou][miou] and [mnet][mnet] (as well as other fundamental libraries). In
fact, we want the ecosystem to be centred around our cooperative so that it
remains resilient and of high quality.

## Libraries and organizations

Most of the projects we are developing are available on the
[packages][packages] page. This page lists all our libraries and tools, which
are designed for use in the development and deployment of unikernels.

One aspect we haven't covered in detail in this tutorial is that most of these
libraries (such as [ocaml-tls][ocaml-tls], [mirage-crypto][mirage-crypto] and
[vif][vif]) are **also** used to develop applications in OCaml (it's not just
about developing unikernels, after all).

This non-exhaustive list may be extended to include projects that contribute to
the unikernel ecosystem but which were not initiated or are not maintained by
our cooperative. The main aim is to provide a central _first entry point_ for
anyone wishing to develop their own unikernel in OCaml.

### How to participe and extend the ecosystem

As you might expect, this website is a unikernel. In this case, it is
[`immuable`][immuable], with a specific archive generated by a program written
with [YOCaml][yocaml], available here: [uniker.nl][uniker.nl].

Adding a new project to this website to expand the OCaml unikernel ecosystem
simply involves adding a Markdown file (with a short YAML description) to our
`./contents/packages/` directory. We then aim to update the archive regularly
so that the site is kept up to date.

Of course (and thanks to YOCaml and [`httpcats`][httpcats]), it is also
possible to have the entire website locally if you wish. Simply install the
necessary dependencies for `uniker.nl` and run this command:

```shell
$ dune exec bin/uniker.exe -- serve 8080
```

So don't be shy – give things a go! Our co-operative can also take back control
of certain libraries that are important to the ecosystem, as has been the case
in the past. Our work over the last 10 years has been driven above all by the
trust that others place in us to carry out our work and to sustain libraries
and projects.

## Use unikernels!

These last few chapters have focused on the development of unikernels, which,
from a developer's perspective, is undeniably exciting and interesting - we can
all agree on that. But we tend to forget all too quickly that, once we've
developed unikernels, we also need to use them. It is, in fact, here that we
encounter the greatest difficulties.

The main aim of our cooperative is to provide unikernels that can replace
services we have for too long left in the hands of companies. Of course, to
provide these unikernels, we must first develop them; but - and this is where
the real difficulty lies - we must also be able to deploy and use them and,
above all, put them to the test on the internet, over time and in real-world
use.

Thus, the tutorial series is just as (if not more) important as the development
of unikernels. It explains how to deploy unikernels, in particular using
[Albatross][albatross], how to establish a _supply chain_ that guarantees the
reproducibility of unikernels (and allows us to be proactive about security
vulnerabilities and easily update our services when one is discovered), and how
to monitor unikernels (who is visiting my website, how much memory is my
unikernel using, etc.).

### Use our services!

Our co-operative doesn't just develop unikernels; we also use them to ensure a
certain standard of quality in the libraries we develop. It is only through
actual use that we manage to uncover the strangest bugs - the ones that can
bring an entire infrastructure to a standstill.

That is why our cooperative maintains its own infrastructure, which is
primarily based on unikernels. This website is proof of that (it is a
unikernel) but we have several other unikernels that enable us to manage, for
example, our domain names, our Let's Encrypt certificates and even our emails
(or even our OPAM packages!).

As such, if you're interested in any of these services, please get in touch and
we'll be delighted to help you either use our infrastructure or deploy your
own, drawing on our expertise.

## A full-stack engineer

The term is indeed appropriate once you realise that you can mentally retrace
the execution path from the Ethernet frame to the handler for your HTTP
request. But it also encompasses aspects relating to deployment and system
administration.

The real challenge with unikernels is that the concept brings two areas of
expertise even closer together: service development and deployment (as well as
the maintenance of these services). It is said that the unikernel lies at the
intersection of these two areas of expertise.

This is how the term _full stack_ should be understood. Beyond the idea of the
unikernel, the concept leads us to a complete re-examination of what the life
cycle of a piece of software can entail: from its development through to its
deployment and eventual use.
